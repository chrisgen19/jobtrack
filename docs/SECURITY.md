during the review; everything else is stated as a design control or a residual risk.

## Residual risks and follow-ups

1. **No antivirus scanning.** Uploads are type- and signature-checked but not scanned. The `BLOCKED` document
   status exists for a scanner (e.g. ClamAV in the worker) to use. Files are only ever downloaded as attachments.
2. **CSP allows `'unsafe-inline'` scripts**, required by Next.js's inline bootstrap without a nonce setup. A
   nonce-based CSP via `proxy.ts` would tighten this.
3. **Per-process rate limits** for uploads/downloads/exports and the extension API (in memory). With several web replicas each enforces
   its own window. Auth limits are shared through the database.
4. **Server Actions are not rate limited** beyond requiring a session. Abuse by a signed-in user only affects their
   own workspace.
5. **Email and Google Calendar were not exercised against the real providers** in this environment (no
   credentials). Their clients are covered with mocked HTTP. Verify after configuring credentials.
6. **Google OAuth verification**: publishing the app to arbitrary Google users may require Google's OAuth
   verification for the calendar scopes.
7. **Dependency updates**: no automated dependency scanning is configured yet (consider Dependabot or Renovate
   plus `pnpm audit` in CI).
8. **Extension tokens do not expire.** They stay valid until the user disconnects them or deletes the account
   (like a "remember me" session). Removing an ID from `EXTENSION_IDS` blocks new connections but not existing
   tokens.
9. **Backups retain deleted data** until they expire (documented in `docs/BACKUP_AND_RECOVERY.md`).
